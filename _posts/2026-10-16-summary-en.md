This method, as reported by MIT neuroscientists, involves randomly interconnected neuron-like units governed by shared plasticity rules. These units are capable of assembling learning motifs and learning pattern recognition and image classification, demonstrating performance similar to backpropagation-trained networks. The key distinction lies in designing a learning circuit directly versus specifying local rules from which the learning machinery can organize itself. This development is significant as it explores the possibility of biological plausibility rather than aiming to replace backpropagation in large-scale models.

**「Consequence for Users and Developers」** The development of a novel approach to artificial neural network learning machinery that self-organizes learning motifs presents a significant advancement in the field. Users of these networks may benefit from improved pattern recognition and image classification capabilities, although the application to biological brains remains speculative.
