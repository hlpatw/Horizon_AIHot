A sophisticated intrusion campaign has been identified targeting Check Point security management servers. The attackers exploited vulnerabilities in the HFS server, utilizing JavaScript’s Math.random\(\) function to reconstruct the internal state of the V8 PRNG algorithm and forge admin session cookies. This campaign highlights the advanced techniques used by cybercriminals and raises concerns about the security of critical infrastructure.

**「Background on Check Point Security Management Servers Intrusion Campaign」** Check Point Security Management Servers are a critical component of cybersecurity infrastructure, providing security solutions for organizations. An intrusion campaign was discovered targeting these servers, with confirmed attack activity between October 17th and November 2nd, 2026. The campaign impacted at least 22 distinct organizations globally. The attack leveraged a vulnerability in the V8 PRNG algorithm, allowing attackers to reconstruct the internal state of the algorithm and recover a session-cookie signing key, thereby forging a signed admin session cookie. This method of attack highlights the sophistication of the attackers and the potential implications for cybersecurity.

**「Impact on Cybersecurity」** The intrusion campaign against Check Point security management servers highlights the increasing sophistication of cyber attacks. This event underscores the importance of robust cybersecurity measures, particularly in protecting critical infrastructure, and may lead to a heightened focus on securing random number generators and other foundational security components.

**「Community Discussion」** Community discussions focus on the complexity of the attack, with concerns raised about the potential for similar techniques to be used by a wider range of cybercriminals. There is also a debate on whether the Math.random\(\) function should be improved to enhance security.
